Why does every election feel like an earthquake?

In Serbia, a movement that started with students after a train station canopy collapsed in Novi Sad and killed 16 people just won Sunday's election, with the highest turnout in more than 30 years, ending the governing party's 14 years in power. In Brazil, the presidential runoff came down to a few points on the very same day. And in Israel, people turned out on Tuesday at a record pace, and the exit polls are so close that the final count will take days. And in France, student protests over teacher shortages and run-down schools have grown into a nationwide movement, with about 550,000 people marching on a single Saturday, while the government fights over its budget ahead of next spring's presidential election.

And here at home, nearly 33 million people have already voted in the midterms before Election Day next Tuesday, November 3rd, more than a quarter of everyone who voted in the last midterms. There's also a record number of women running for state legislatures… 3,824 of them, more than ever before.

So what's really going on? The old structures are being put to a vote, everywhere at once. People who haven't voted in years are showing up, young people are leading, and the margins are razor thin, because the collective is deciding which way we go next. It's the same thing that happens to a client right before her primary pattern clears… it gets louder, it gets uncomfortable, and then it finally moves.

So if your body feels it, if you're anxious or wired or tired of checking your phone, that's the collective energy moving through you. Be sure to care for your nervous system, and don't hold on to any energy that's not actually yours or that isn't going to serve you.

The good news: Dr. Honoka Kiso is paving the way for our next evolution… regrowing teeth

Imagine being born without some of your adult teeth, and being told your only options are implants or dentures for the rest of your life. Dr. Honoka Kiso is the co-founder and CEO of Toregem Biopharma, a Kyoto University spin-out building a drug designed to help the body grow those teeth on its own.

The first safety study in adults is done, and the company says it was safe. Now the drug is heading into its first trial in 24 children who were born missing teeth, and the goal is to bring it to patients by 2030. It hasn't been shown to regrow human teeth yet… but a woman leading a company that's trying to help the body regenerate what it lost? That's our next evolution.
