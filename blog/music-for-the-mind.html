Music for Stress Relief: How Soft Acoustic Music Calms Your Brain

By Tamara RoseFounder, The Beyond Human MethodUpdated October 10, 20263 minute read

One of the gentlest ways I know to find calm in a busy day is soft acoustic music. A quiet guitar, a piano or a soothing voice can soften a stressful day and help you drift toward sleep, and research on music, stress and sleep supports what you've probably felt for years.

Why acoustic music feels so soothing

Acoustic music is made with natural instruments like guitar, piano, strings and soft vocals. The slower tempos, warm tones and simple harmonies give your mind something steady to follow, and I find that steadiness is a big part of why it relaxes me.

Music and your stress response

In a 2013 study published in PLOS ONE, researchers at the University of Zurich found that women who listened to relaxing music before a stressful task recovered faster afterward on one marker of the autonomic nervous system, the part of you that handles stress and rest. The results were mixed on other measures, which is a good reminder that music supports calm without being a cure for stress.

Music and sleep

A 2008 study in the Journal of Advanced Nursing found that students with sleep complaints who listened to relaxing classical music for 45 minutes at bedtime for three weeks reported meaningfully better sleep quality than those who didn't. Soft music works well as part of a wind-down routine, especially when it replaces scrolling or the TV.

Music and your emotions

You've probably felt a song change your mood in seconds. Choosing gentle music on purpose lets you use that power to settle yourself instead of being pulled along by whatever is playing around you.

How to use music to calm down

  • Choose slow and simple. I look for slow, unhurried music with few lyrics or soft vocals.
  • Make a wind-down playlist. Play it at the same time each night so your body learns the cue.
  • Pair it with breath. Let your breathing slow to match the music.
  • Keep the volume low. Gentle background sound is more relaxing than music that demands your attention.

Common questions

What kind of music is best for stress relief?

Slow, gentle music with simple melodies, such as soft acoustic guitar, piano or calming classical pieces, is a lovely place to start.

Does music before bed help you sleep?

Research suggests relaxing music at bedtime can improve sleep quality, especially when it's part of a consistent wind-down routine.

How long should I listen to calming music?

Even ten to fifteen minutes can help you unwind; one sleep study used 45 minutes at bedtime for three weeks.

Sources

  1. Thoma, M. V., et al., University of Zurich (2013). The effect of music on the human stress response. PLOS ONE.
  2. Harmat, L., Takács, J., & Bódizs, R. (2008). Music improves sleep quality in students. Journal of Advanced Nursing.
