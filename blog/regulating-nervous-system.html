How to Regulate Your Nervous System When Everything Feels Like Too Much

By Tamara RoseFounder, The Beyond Human MethodUpdated October 10, 20263 minute read

If you're juggling work, family and everything else on your plate, there's a good chance your nervous system rarely gets a break. Screens, notifications, email and the pressure to do it all keep you in a state of constant alertness, and learning how to regulate your nervous system is how you find the calm underneath the chaos without having to step away from your life.

Why so many of us feel overstimulated

Your nervous system has two main gears. The sympathetic side gets you ready for action, the fight or flight response, and the parasympathetic side lets you rest, digest and recover. Modern life keeps pressing the gas with phones, notifications and inboxes that never empty, and research going back to a 2001 study in an American Psychological Association journal shows that switching between tasks costs you time and focus every time you do it. When you never shift back into rest, you end up wired, tired and reactive.

Signs your nervous system needs support

  • You feel tired but can't switch off at night.
  • Small things set you off or bring you to tears.
  • You reach for your phone the moment there's a pause.
  • Your shoulders, jaw or stomach are tight most of the day.

Six practices that help regulate your nervous system

  1. Meditation and mindfulness. The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health notes that mindfulness-based practices may help with anxiety, depression and sleep. A few minutes a day is enough to begin.
  2. Slow breathing. A 2023 Stanford study found that five minutes a day of breathing with long exhales improved mood and lowered breathing rate. Try a breath break two or three times a day.
  3. Time in nature. A 2019 University of Michigan study found that twenty to thirty minutes in nature lowered cortisol, and grounding barefoot adds another layer.
  4. Less screen time. A 2018 University of Pennsylvania study found that limiting social media to about 30 minutes a day reduced loneliness and depression within a few weeks. Turning off non-essential notifications is a simple start.
  5. Regular movement. Moving your body is one of my favorite ways to work stress through instead of holding it. Gentle counts: walking, stretching or dancing in the kitchen.
  6. Protecting your sleep. Sleep is when you recover from the day. A consistent bedtime, a dark room and no screens before bed make a real difference.

When regulation isn't enough

These practices calm your system day to day. If you notice that the same stress keeps coming back no matter how well you take care of yourself, it's worth looking at what's underneath it. Often a pattern keeps your body bracing for something long after the original reason is gone, and that's where the deeper work begins.

Common questions

How can I calm my nervous system quickly?

Slow your breathing so the exhale is longer than the inhale, for example in for four and out for six or eight, for a few minutes. Stepping outside or placing a hand on your heart can help too.

What does an overstimulated nervous system feel like?

Often tired but wired, easily irritated, restless, unable to switch off at night, with tension in the jaw, shoulders or stomach.

How long does it take to regulate your nervous system?

You can feel a shift in minutes with breathing, and daily practices build a steadier baseline over weeks. If the same stress keeps returning, look at the pattern underneath it.
