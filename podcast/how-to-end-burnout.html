How to End Burnout for Good: Getting Out of the Crash and Burn Cycle

A conversation with Tamara RoseNovember 5, 202568 minutesOriginally aired as Divine Feminine Radio, Episode 39

If your weeks run on go, go, go until you crash on the weekend, you're living in a burnout cycle, and it can last a week, a month or a whole year. This episode is about how that cycle works, and how to tend to your nervous system so you stop running on empty.

What you'll hear

Nervous system regulation is the first thing Tamara works on with every client, because you can't create big change in your life while your nervous system is strung out. She lived it herself before she started teaching it about five years ago, and once clients felt the difference, it became part of the methodology.

In this episode she explains how the burnout cycle works, where your energy is quietly draining, and the simple practices that keep you out of the crash.

How the burnout cycle works

You go and do until you can't, then you crash and recover. For many people the cycle is five to seven days, with the weekend spent vegging out; for others it's a month, or most of a year followed by a long stretch of doing nothing, and the recovery often takes almost as long as the push. Tamara, a born type A, recognized the belief underneath it in herself: "I'll rest when I'm done." Done might mean the end of the to-do list, the house project or retirement, but there's always something new at the front of the line, so you never get there.

We're trained for it young. School taught us that attendance mattered most, to eat when the bell rang and to push through anything short of a fever, and that pattern carries straight into the nine to five. The new perspective Tamara offers is to build moments of vacation, respite or retreat into your days, weeks and months, so you live from a full cup instead of waiting to refill an empty one.

The hidden drains on your energy

Burnout comes from more than your schedule. It's also the information you take in all day, the pings, texts, emails and ads in your feed, each one asking your brain to sort and decide, along with the mental load of small decisions running in the background. Clutter counts too; your brain learns to tune out the blanket on the couch or the hundreds of unread emails, but they still take up space. All of it is energy going out, even when you don't notice it.

Then there's resistance, which Tamara calls one of the biggest drains we never think about: the conversation you're avoiding with your boss, the job you want to quit, the relationship you keep tolerating. Listen for "I have to" or "I don't want to, but I'll do it anyway." Changing the words to "I get to" won't help much if the feeling underneath is still contraction.

Surrender, change course or zoom out

Her first answer to resistance is to make a choice. Either surrender to where you are, even 24 hours at a time, or make a plan and change course; there is always a choice, and believing you have none keeps you in victim energy. Her second answer is to zoom out. Up close, an argument over dishes in the kitchen holds a lot of drama and energy, but zoomed out you can ask why you'd attract it and see the opportunity, like learning to ask for what you need. That shift is much easier when your nervous system is calm, because fight or flight only shows you more things to fight.

Daily practices to regenerate

Start by noticing your own cycle, how long you go and how long you need to recover. Then build daily and monthly routines that keep you balanced, so you regenerate in 20 or 30 minutes instead of 48 hours. Tamara's top few are a warm bath before bed, because submerging the whole body calms the nervous system; cutting out alcohol; eight to nine hours of sleep for women, with no scrolling at night; and meditation, which can take you into theta brain waves that sometimes rejuvenate you more than sleep. After a stretch of heavy travel, a 30-minute meditation lying down did more for her than an hour and a half nap would have.

And stop saving yourself for later, because later doesn't exist. If the vacation is waiting for someday, decide where you're going and put it on the calendar. Tamara leaves listeners with a possibility, that you could love your life, keep your nervous system regulated and never hit burnout again. Want to go further?
