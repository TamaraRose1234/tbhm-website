I Know What I Need to Do, So Why Can't I Do It?

By Tamara RoseUpdated October 1, 20265 minute read

You know you need to raise your prices, leave the relationship, start the business, go to bed on time or stop saying yes to everyone. You could write the plan in your sleep. And somehow, week after week, you don't do it.

This is the single most common thing I hear from the women I work with, and almost all of them have quietly decided it means something is wrong with them. Nothing is wrong with you. There's a reason the gap exists, and once you see it, you'll stop trying to close it with more effort.

Discipline was never the missing piece

If willpower were the answer, you'd have solved this years ago. High-achieving women are usually extraordinarily disciplined; you've used discipline to build careers, raise kids and hold entire families together. So when discipline doesn't work here, it's a clue that you're working on the wrong layer.

Your system is keeping you safe

Every pattern you live by was once a solution. "If I keep everyone happy, I'm safe." "If I never ask for much, I won't be disappointed." Your nervous system learned those rules early and has been faithfully running them ever since.

When you try to do the new thing, like charging more or asking for help, your body reads it as danger, because it breaks a rule that kept you safe. You don't experience that as fear, usually. You experience it as being tired, getting busy with something else, forgetting, or deciding to do it next week. That's your system pulling you back to what's familiar, and it's very good at it.

Why more information makes it worse

When we can't do something, we tend to study it: another book, another course, another podcast about the thing we already understand. It feels productive, and it widens the gap, because now you know even more and you're doing exactly the same. Understanding lives in your thoughts, while the pattern lives in your body and your energy, and you can't think your way out of something you feel your way into.

What actually closes the gap

The gap closes when the pattern pulling you back is cleared, and when your body has practiced the new reality long enough to stop treating it as a threat. In practice that looks like three things:

  1. Name the rule. What does doing this thing break? Finish the sentence "If I do this, then…" and be honest about the ending.
  2. Calm the system first. Change won't hold while your nervous system is braced. A short daily practice that brings your body back to calm makes the next step feel possible.
  3. Take the uncomfortable action, small. Expect it to feel wrong. The discomfort doesn't mean stop; it's the feeling of leaving the familiar, and it fades as you keep going.

Those three steps will move you, and they work much faster once the root pattern underneath is cleared, because then there's nothing left pulling you back. I explain how that works in the full guide, why you keep repeating the same pattern.
